# Smart Events: Technology & AI

## 1. The commercial event-tech stack in 2026

**Market structure.** Event Tech Live's 2026 stack model splits the market into five layers: (1) registration/identity, (2) on-site engagement and data capture, (3) content/session management, (4) analytics and revenue intelligence, (5) integration and data orchestration (https://eventtechlive.com/the-event-tech-stack-you-need-in-2026-from-registration-to-revenue-intelligence/). Their framing is worth internalising: registration is no longer a ticketing function, it is the identity layer for the entire event. That means the registration platform choice determines what analytics and CRM attribution are possible later.

**Enterprise suites.** Skift Meetings reports Gartner's 2026 event-tech rankings placing Cvent, RainFocus and vFairs as Leaders, Bizzabo as a Visionary, and Stova as a Niche Player (Stova declined to participate) (https://meetings.skift.com/2026/04/01/gartners-2026-event-tech-rankings/). Cvent has consolidated aggressively under Blackstone ownership: iCapture and Jifflenow (Jan 2024), Reposite (Jun 2024), Splash (Sep 2024) (https://meetings.skift.com/2024/09/04/cvents-spending-streak-continues-with-splash-acquisition/). Practical read: **Splash is now Cvent's field-marketing tier, not an independent alternative.** RainFocus occupies the mega-conference/multi-event-portfolio end with the deepest data model but the highest implementation overhead — it expects dedicated event-tech engineering resource. Bizzabo differentiates on the Klik SmartBadge wearable for proximity/session analytics. Stova is the closest structural analogue to Cvent for venue sourcing plus physical hardware.

**Networking/matchmaking layer.** Swapcard, Brella and Grip are the specialists — Swapcard for trade-show/exhibitor matchmaking and lead scoring sold to sponsors, Brella for intent-based matchmaking and 1:1 meeting scheduling, Grip for AI matchmaking on large exhibition floors. Whova is mid-market with the broadest engagement toolkit. Luma and Eventbrite are self-serve tools — Event Tech Live positions them as adequate under ~100 attendees, not for MNC programmes. Sessionize handles call-for-papers and speaker-submission-to-agenda automation; Sessionboard, Sched and Lineup Ninja compete in that layer.

**Hopin's breakup — and why it matters.** Hopin sold its Events and Session products to RingCentral in August 2023 for $15M upfront plus up to $35M contingent, after raising ~$1B at a $7.75B peak valuation (https://meetings.skift.com/2023/08/09/hopin-events-and-session-products-sold-for-15-million/). It sold the remainder — StreamYard, Streamable, Superwave — to Bending Spoons in April 2024 (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240409133596/en/Bending-Spoons-to-Acquire-StreamYard-Leader-in-Live-Streaming-and-Video-Recording-Solutions). The products survive as RingCentral Events/Sessions. **Governance lesson for enterprise contracts: virtual-event platform vendors can change owners mid-contract, so insist on data-export rights and escrow-style continuity clauses.**

**On-site hardware/software.** Badge printing standardises on Zebra card/label printers (the ZC10L / ZC10L-Pro prints edge-to-edge large-format badges and can encode UHF RFID and NFC inline — https://www.zebra.com/us/en/products/printers/card/zc10l.html), with Epson and Brother as alternates. RFID/NFC credentials for access control and session tracking are now mainstream for corporate conferences. Facial-recognition check-in is shipped, not theoretical: fielddrive (Belgium) is the named specialist, with vFairs and InEvent also offering it — see §5 before you deploy it. Lead retrieval: Captello (6,000+ integrations), Cvent iCapture, Eventbase.

> **Critical operational filter — offline check-in.** Platforms with mature onsite modules (Cvent OnArrival, fielddrive, Jonas Event Technology, Holon, ADITUS, Entegy, Bizzabo Klik) support degraded-network check-in; several modern SaaS platforms — reported as including RainFocus, Swoogo, SpotMe, Webex Events, Whova, vFairs, Swapcard — **require persistent internet for check-in** (https://eventtechlive.com/event-registration-platforms-the-2026-landscape/). Treat this as a hard gate in vendor selection for hotel ballrooms and convention centres with contended wifi.

**Streaming/broadcast.** Production switching: vMix, OBS Studio, Wirecast (Telestream); multi-destination via Restream. Delivery: Mux for polished HLS pipelines, Cloudflare Stream for cheap egress at the edge, AWS Elemental MediaLive for 24/7 channels, AWS IVS for low-latency inside AWS. Interactive/conversational latency: LiveKit (self-hostable WebRTC), Daily, 100ms, Agora. LL-HLS at 2–5s is the 2026 default for broadcast-style streams where CDN economics matter; WebRTC/SFU is sub-500ms for genuinely interactive formats (https://leanopstech.com/blog/mux-vs-cloudflare-stream-vs-cloudfront-2026/).

**Integration reality.** Cvent exposes a unified Platform REST API with OAuth 2.0 client credentials, plus legacy SOAP/BadgeKit/CSN APIs, downloadable OpenAPI specs, webhooks and white-label docs (https://developers.cvent.com/docs/rest-api/overview). Cvent SSO supports SAML and OpenID Connect for planner login, access portals, registrants and Attendee Hub (https://developers.cvent.com/docs/sso/overview). Cvent's Salesforce app syncs on a schedule **unless** you configure the Salesforce OAuth integration, which enables real-time attendee sync (https://developers.cvent.com/docs/cvent-salesforce-app/salesforce-oauth). Cvent, RainFocus, Bizzabo, Stova, Eventsforce, inwink, RefTech and Captello all claim native Salesforce or HubSpot coupling, though quality varies; Swoogo advertises 30+ native integrations plus an open API; Cvent integration is broad but often requires middleware (Zapier/Workato).

**The acceptance test:** does data flow bidirectionally in near-real-time, or is it a batch export after the show?

## 2. AI in events: shipped vs marketed

### Shipped and load-bearing

- **Real-time translation and captioning** — the most mature category. KUDO's 2025 industry data claims 68% of global conference organisers now use real-time AI translation, up from under 20% three years earlier, with AI-translated meetings up 200% YoY (https://eventtechlive.com/breaking-the-language-barrier-multilingual-conference-technology-in-2026/, https://kudo.ai/). Wordly runs 60+ target languages in a single AI-only session (https://www.wordly.ai/). Interprefy remains the RSI incumbent. The 2026 operating pattern is hybrid: human interpreters for high-stakes/legal sessions, AI for breadth and breakouts. *The 68% figure is vendor-published — directional only.*
- **Matchmaking and networking recommendations** — Brella, Grip, RainFocus and Bizzabo all use registration data plus engagement signals to recommend meetings and sessions.
- **Session summarisation and content repurposing** — genuinely working: transcripts → summaries → social snippets → highlight reels. Snapsight shipped "Remix" to turn live sessions into branded marketing assets (https://briefglance.com/articles/snapsights-remix-ai-turns-live-events-into-instant-content-engines). Practitioner demand is measured: multilingual transcripts 58%, subtitles/dubbing 53%, AI meeting notes 51%, marketing-ready summaries 50% (https://www.efficientlyconnected.com/ai-translation-events-content-engine-wordly-2026/).
- **Organiser and attendee copilots** — Bizzabo shipped Event OS Copilot in November 2024 and "Bizzy," an attendee-facing copilot pre-loaded with event data, in April 2026 (vendor source). RainFocus launched "Nexus" AI agents in January 2026 for predictive attendance/risk forecasting.
- **RFP and proposal drafting** — Cvent's CventIQ generates RFP proposals; Cvent reports ~200 staff dedicated to AI (competitor-published, directional only).
- **Computer-vision crowd/dwell analytics** — commercially available: Exposure Analytics "Apex" (heatmaps, foot traffic, dwell time), The Trade Group "ExhibitIQ" (https://marketing.tradegroup.com/exhibitiq).
- **Sponsor-ROI analytics** — Premagic reframes sponsorship as measurable attendee-distributed content, giving sponsors real-time dashboards of views/clicks/shares/downloads.

### Marketing-forward — verify before buying

"AI-native" and "world's first AI agent for events" positioning (Envelope, Zuddl, run.events) is largely reg-page/agenda generation wrapped in agent language. **Emotion-detection from facial expressions and "crowd energy" sensing are being sold but are scientifically contested and legally hazardous in the EU.** Event Tech Live's bluntest line: what AI is not doing well, despite vendor claims, is replacing the strategic judgement experienced event professionals bring. Agenda personalisation is real but shallow — mostly collaborative filtering over track/tag data, not deep reasoning. AI agents for registration support are mostly RAG chatbots over FAQ + agenda: useful, but not autonomous.

## 3. Engineering stack to *build* a smart-event product

**Languages/frameworks.** TypeScript + Next.js for registration microsites, attendee web app and organiser console (server components for SEO-visible agenda pages, edge rendering for global attendee bases). Python + FastAPI for the AI/data services (transcription orchestration, matchmaking, RAG). Go for high-throughput realtime services and media control planes — LiveKit is Go, Owncast is Go, ntfy is Go. **Elixir/Phoenix is the strongest fit for presence-heavy realtime** (Phoenix Channels + Presence handle tens of thousands of concurrent attendees per node). Mobile: React Native or Flutter for cross-platform attendee apps; **Kotlin/Swift where you need reliable NFC, background BLE beacons and hardware-grade offline scanning** (pretix's own scanner app is native Kotlin).

**Realtime transport.** WebSocket for bidirectional live polling/Q&A/chat; **SSE for one-way agenda-change and announcement fanout** (cheaper, survives proxies better); WebRTC for interactive video and 1:1 networking rooms; **MQTT for venue IoT** — occupancy sensors, beacons, door controllers, environmental telemetry — because it tolerates flaky links and low-power devices.

**QR/NFC and badge printing.** Browser scanning via `zxing-js/library` or `html5-qrcode`, with the native `BarcodeDetector` API where available (faster, no WASM payload). Native NFC via `react-native-nfc-manager`. NDEF encoding for writable badges. Badge printing: generate ZPL for Zebra directly (`zpl-image` converts raster art to ZPL GRF/Z64), ESC/POS for receipt-style printers. **Print locally over a LAN print service, never round-trip to the cloud per badge.**

**Offline-first check-in — the non-negotiable.** Venue wifi is contended, crew LTE is unreliable in basements and halls, and a check-in queue that stalls is a visible failure in front of the client's CEO. Pattern: PWA or native app that pre-downloads the full attendee manifest (hashed ticket secrets, not PII where avoidable) into IndexedDB (via Dexie.js) or SQLite; validate scans locally; queue check-in events; **sync via CRDT (Yjs or Automerge) so multiple desks converge without a server round-trip and without double-admit races.** Server-authoritative deduplication runs on reconnect. IndexedDB quota is not guaranteed and can be evicted under storage pressure, which is a genuine argument for native + SQLite at large scale.

**Push.** APNs/FCM for attendee apps; Web Push for PWAs; self-hosted ntfy for crew/ops alerting (run-of-show cues, incident escalation) where you don't want attendee-app infrastructure in the loop.

**AI plumbing.** LLM APIs behind your own gateway so you can swap models and enforce redaction. RAG over agenda, speaker bios, exhibitor catalogue, floor plan and FAQ — **chunk by session/exhibitor entity, not by page**, and re-index on every agenda change. Vector store: pgvector if you already run Postgres (strongly preferred — one datastore, one backup story), otherwise Qdrant/Weaviate/Pinecone. Transcription: Whisper (self-host for data-residency-sensitive client content), Deepgram or AssemblyAI for managed low-latency streaming ASR. Translation: DeepL or the ASR vendor's pipeline; Wordly/KUDO/Interprefy if you want a turnkey event-grade product rather than a build. **MCP servers are the emerging integration pattern** — wrap your registration, agenda and CRM APIs as MCP tools so an organiser-facing agent can answer "who from Account X hasn't registered?" without bespoke glue. LiveKit Agents (Apache-2.0, Python) is the reference framework for realtime voice concierge.

### Licensed vs unlicensed — the legal point

**A repo with no LICENSE file is not open source**: default copyright applies and all rights are reserved. GitHub's terms grant only the right to view and fork *within GitHub*, not to use, modify or redistribute. Concrete examples found in this sweep: `featherbear/expo-nfc-react-native` (no licence, last push 2020-07-21) and `osbridge/open-source-bridge-proposals` (no licence). **Do not vendor either into client work.**

Three categories to keep separate in procurement:

1. **Permissive (MIT/Apache-2.0/ISC)** — Indico, LiveKit, Jitsi, Owncast, mediasoup, ntfy, Dexie, Yjs, whisper. Safe to embed in proprietary client deliverables with attribution.
2. **Copyleft / network copyleft (GPL/LGPL/AGPL)** — BigBlueButton, OBS, Mobilizon, Gancio, Hi.Events, pretix, pretalx. **AGPL is the one to watch:** if you host a modified version for a client, you owe source to that client's users.
3. **Source-available, i.e. *not* OSS despite the GitHub badge** — Cal.com's `ee/` directory, and pretix/pretalx's additional terms banning third-party SaaS. **These are the exact traps for an agency reselling hosted capability.**

## 5. Data, privacy and compliance

**GDPR and biometrics.** Facial recognition becomes regulated biometric data under GDPR Article 9 the moment software is used to identify or verify a person — not merely when a face is photographed. Processing is prohibited unless an Article 9(2) condition is met, with explicit consent the usual route; a pre-ticked box does not qualify (https://www.gdpr-advisor.com/gdpr-and-facial-recognition-privacy-implications-and-legal-considerations/, https://didit.me/blog/biometric-consent-gdpr-compliance/). Two consequences that bite at events:

- The Swedish school and Spanish supermarket cases establish that **consent obtained under a power imbalance or without genuine choice is invalid** — so for **employee** town halls and internal leadership events, consent-based facial check-in is weak, and you need a non-biometric alternative queue of *equal convenience*.
- Exposure is up to €20M or 4% of global turnover.

**Practical stance:** offer FR check-in as strictly opt-in with a QR/NFC fallback, capture consent as a separate affirmative act at registration and re-confirm on site, delete face templates on a defined short retention clock, and run a DPIA before the event. Photography/videography needs its own layered notice — signage at entry, a notice in the registration flow, and a no-photo lanyard or badge marker that the crew is briefed to honour.

**India's DPDP Act 2023 + DPDP Rules 2025.** MeitY notified the Rules on 14 November 2025 with a phased runway: Data Protection Board constituted 13 Nov 2025; consent-manager registration from 13 Nov 2026; **full obligations — itemised consent notices, data-principal rights, breach notification, Significant Data Fiduciary duties — from 13 May 2027** (https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf, https://www.ey.com/en_in/insights/cybersecurity/transforming-data-privacy-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025). Operationally for Indian-hosted MNC events: a **standalone**, plain-language notice separate from T&Cs, itemising exactly what attendee data is collected and why; a named mechanism to withdraw consent that is as easy as giving it; purpose-based retention timelines rather than indefinite CRM dumping; **breach notification within 72 hours**; verifiable parental consent for under-18s (relevant to family days and CSR events); and impact assessments for biometrics, AI-driven screening, continuous monitoring, geolocation tracking and large-scale cross-border sharing (https://www.scrut.io/post/dpdp-rules). **May 2027 is close enough that registration microsites built now should be built to it.**

**Data residency.** For EU-attendee events, prefer vendors with EU hosting regions and documented SCCs; for India, expect MNC clients to start asking where attendee data lands. This is where self-hosted open source has real commercial value: Indico, Jitsi, LiveKit, Owncast and whisper.cpp can all run inside a client's own cloud tenancy, converting a cross-border transfer problem into a contractual one.

**SOC 2 / ISO 27001 as a procurement gate.** Enterprise and healthcare buyers increasingly treat SOC 2 as contractual, refusing vendors without a SOC 2 report or acceptable equivalent such as ISO 27001. Current vendor posture: **Cvent** — ISO 27001, ISO 27701, SOC 1 and SOC 2, PCI DSS, TLS 1.2 in transit / AES-256 at rest, with a Trust Center (https://www.cvent.com/en/security, https://trust.cvent.com/). **Bizzabo** — SOC 2 Type 2, ISO 27001, PCI DSS SAQ D v4, positioned as processor (https://www.bizzabo.com/security). **Swapcard** — SOC 2 Type 2 and ISO 27001 processes, AES-256, full-time CISO and DPO, CREST-certified penetration testing (https://www.swapcard.com/platform/security).

### A five-question gate before any platform enters a client programme

1. Does check-in work **fully offline**?
2. Is there a real bidirectional API and SAML/OIDC SSO, or only batch CSV?
3. **SOC 2 Type 2 *report*** (not "SOC 2 processes") plus ISO 27001, obtainable under NDA?
4. Data residency region, sub-processor list, and deletion SLA **in writing**?
5. For any biometric or CV component — DPIA done, opt-in architecture, template retention clock, and a non-biometric path of *equal convenience*?

For anything you build in-house, add a sixth: **licence class of every OSS dependency recorded in an SBOM, with AGPL and source-available components flagged before they reach a client deployment.**
