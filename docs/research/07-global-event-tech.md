# Global event-technology landscape and research frontier: India, China, Japan, Hong Kong, Germany, UAE

> Research brief · compiled 2026-07-29

Scope: the vendor market, the trade-show calendar, the peer-reviewed research frontier, and the regulatory envelope for enterprise event delivery in six markets. Every factual claim carries a source URL. Claims I could not trace to a primary document are marked **UNVERIFIED**.

## 1. Per-region vendor and platform landscape

### India
The Indian enterprise stack is bifurcated: global platforms for MNC-mandated programmes, India-origin platforms for cost-sensitive and regional work. Two India-founded virtual/hybrid platforms achieved real scale — Hubilo raised USD 125M led by Alkeon Capital in Oct 2021, taking total funding to USD 153M (https://www.business-standard.com/article/companies/virtual-events-platform-hubilo-raises-125-mn-for-global-expansion-121101201064_1.html — this URL now returns HTTP 403 to automated fetching; the round is independently confirmed as a USD 125M Series B led by Alkeon Capital with Lightspeed Venture Partners and Balderton Capital, announced 11–12 Oct 2021, bringing total funding to USD 153M: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-new-era-of-events-is-unfolding-before-us-as-hubilo-announces-125-million-in-series-b-investment-301397081.html and https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/11/hubilo-picks-up-125m-to-double-down-on-building-a-more-engaging-platform-for-virtual-and-live-events/), and Airmeet raised a USD 35M Series B with Prosus Ventures, Sistema Asia Fund, RingCentral Ventures and KDDI Open Innovation Fund (https://venturebeat.com/2022/02/08/virtual-events-platform-airmeet-lands-35m). Both pivoted heavily toward webinar/demand-gen rather than large-format conferences; **UNVERIFIED** whether either retains meaningful enterprise conference share in 2026 — I found no 2026 primary filings or product announcements.

Venue-side, the structural change is capacity: Bharat Mandapam (IECC Pragati Maidan) and Yashobhoomi (IICC Dwarka) now anchor large-format work — SATTE 2027 moves to Yashobhoomi with 37,000+ gross sqm (https://www.satte.in/) — all SATTE figures used in §2 were re-confirmed verbatim on satte.in (organiser-reported, not audited). Treat "top event software in India" listicles (technologycounter.com, softwaresuggest.com, techimply.com, evntzo.com) as affiliate lead-generation directories, not market research: mutually citing, no survey instrument, and they list vendors with no Indian delivery presence.

### China
The dominant pattern is not a Western-style event platform but a WeChat Mini Program front-end over a domestic SaaS back-end. **31会议 / 31Event** (https://www.31huiyi.com/) is the clearest enterprise-grade example — founded 2010 (confirmed in the cited 36Kr piece) — and markets itself explicitly as a Cvent alternative for China (https://www.31huiyi.com/h5/news/article?articleId=2352891823). Its 300,000+ institutional clients / 1.3M+ events claim is self-reported marketing: **UNVERIFIED**.

**Two corrections to the sourcing here.** (a) The SaaS pivot date is wrong: the cited 36Kr article dates the "全面升级为了一体化SaaS会议活动管理系统" upgrade to **April 2015**, not 2014. (b) The product family "31轻会, 31大会易, 31展览云, 31智慧现场" is **not supported by the 36Kr citation** — that article is contemporaneous with late 2014 and names entirely different products (`3188.la`, a consumer HTML5 activity-management app, and `31美加` on the B2B side), with metrics as of Dec 2014 (600M cumulative registrations, 600,000 listings, 100,000 organiser clients) and reference customers including the World Internet Conference and the Beijing Marathon. The current product names presumably come from 31huiyi.com, but that site did not return fetchable content on re-check, so the product family is **UNVERIFIED** — do not attribute it to the 36Kr URL. The qualitative framing of 31会议 as "the clearest enterprise-grade example" also rests on no independent market-share data and is **UNVERIFIED**.

Platform-level validation: the World Economic Forum ran its first official WeChat Mini Program powered by Tencent for Summer Davos in Dalian, covering event information, live content and interactive services (https://www.tencent.com/en-us/articles/2202375.html). That is the reference architecture — identity via WeChat OpenID/UnionID, payment and e-fapiao in-channel, no app download. Vhall (微吼) and 活动树 operate in this space but I reached no primary corporate source for either: **UNVERIFIED**.

### Japan
**EventHub** (株式会社EventHub, https://eventhub.so/ and https://eventhub.jp/) founded 2016, HQ Japan with Singapore and Australia presence, is a JAFCO portfolio company (https://www.jafco.co.jp/english/portfolio/eventhub/) and is the closest thing to a domestic B2B enterprise event platform. The market segments cleanly: EventHub for webinar-to-conference unified data, EventRegist for payment-heavy registration, Peatix for community-scale events (https://eventhub.jp/blog/event-platform-comparison/). Peatix and EventHub have a partnership for custom per-event mobile apps (https://blog.peatix.com/news/release/eventhub.html). Practical consequence for an MNC programme in Japan: expect a *separate* Japanese-language registration and comms layer rather than localisation of a global tool.

### Hong Kong
HKTDC is both organiser and platform owner. **Click2Match** is its AI business-matching platform, offered alongside physical fairs under the EXHIBITION+ hybrid model (https://www.hktdc.com/event/exhibitionplus/en). It includes **Scan2Match** — exhibitor QR scanning via the HKTDC Marketplace App, bookmarking, electronic floor plans, post-fair messaging — plus an on-site AI SmartBot generating personalised visit routes (https://www.hktdc.com/event/hkelectronicsfairse/en). Venues: HKCEC and AsiaWorld-Expo (https://www.hktdc.com/event/ecoexpoasia/en/shared-contents/access-to-asiaworld-expo). In HK the *organiser* supplies the tech stack, so an integrator's leverage sits in content, hosted-buyer curation and on-ground execution — not registration systems.

### Germany
Germany is the largest trade-fair economy in the sample: AUMA reports 180 national and international trade fairs annually, 13 million visitors, 180,000+ exhibiting companies, 70 exhibition centres, 3.25 million sqm of hall space, EUR 30bn value creation and 280,000 jobs (https://www.auma.de/en). Every one of these figures was re-confirmed verbatim on auma.de (the page additionally claims EUR 5.4bn in tax revenue). **Caveat that belongs in the text, not just my notes:** these are trade-association self-reported aggregates and **no reference year is attached to any of them except the 3.25M sqm hall-space figure, which auma.de dates to 2026**. The reference period for the visitor, exhibitor, value-creation and jobs numbers is therefore unknown — cite them as "AUMA's standing industry figures", not as a given year's performance. Products here are shaped by GDPR-as-feature. **Converve** (https://www.converve.com/) positions as a GDPR data processor for registration and networking, citing SAP, Cisco, Deutsche Messe and UBM as customers (https://www.converve.com/event-networking-blog/gdpr-for-event-managers-an-expert-opinion/). **vivenu** (https://vivenu.com/about) is a German white-label ticketing API selling on open REST access, 100% data ownership and GDPR compliance, USD 65M raised — confirmed on the About page: founded in **Frankfurt in 2018**, now Düsseldorf and New York, "$65 million in funding" secured in **2021**, positioned as "hyper-flexible" ticketing with "complete control over your data" and (in the page's schema markup) "API-first ticketing infrastructure" / "headless ticketing architecture". **One claim not supported by the cited page: GDPR compliance is nowhere mentioned on vivenu.com/about** — that attribution is **UNVERIFIED** and should be dropped or re-sourced to vivenu's DPA/terms before it goes near a client deck. Note also that USD 65M is a 2021 figure with no later round evidenced, so "USD 65M raised" may understate or overstate current totals. German buying pattern: DPAs, EU hosting and deletion schedules gate the shortlist before any feature comparison.

### UAE / Dubai
Permitting is fully digital and is the binding constraint on delivery: DET (which absorbed DTCM) operates the **ePermits** portal, mandatory for public and ticketed events including conferences and activations, with E-Permit approval a prerequisite for E-Code issuance (https://www.dubaidet.gov.ae/en/licences-and-permits/events-and-activations, https://www.dubaidet.gov.ae/en/our-services/for-consumers-and-students/register-on-epermits). Expo 2020's legacy asset is **Expo City Dubai**, operating Terra, the Connect Conference Centre (C3) and Nexus, marketed as the UAE's first Green Innovation District with a visitor app (https://www.expocitydubai.com/en/). Expo City now also hosts GITEX Global.

## 4. Government and regulatory programmes that bind event delivery

**India — DPDP.** MeitY notified the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 on 13 Nov 2025 vide **G.S.R. 846(E)**, published in the Gazette of India, Extraordinary, Part II, Section 3(i) on **14 Nov 2025**. The phasing is now primary-sourced: the gazette text of Rule 1 was extracted directly and reads — "(2) Rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21 shall come into force on the date of their publication in the Official Gazette. (3) Rule 4 shall come into force one year after the date of publication of this Gazette. (4) Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 shall come into force eighteen months after the date of publication of this Gazette." Rules 17–21 are the Data Protection Board provisions (appointment of Chairperson and Members, functioning of the Board as a digital office, terms of service of Board officers), so "Board provisions immediate" is correct. Rule 4 is Registration and obligations of Consent Manager. The 18-month tranche is the operationally relevant one: Rule 3 (notice by Data Fiduciary to Data Principal), Rule 6 (reasonable security safeguards), Rule 7 (personal data breach intimation), Rule 8 (retention limits), Rules 10–12 (verifiable consent for children), Rule 13 (Significant Data Fiduciary obligations), Rule 14 (Data Principal rights) and Rule 15 (transfer of personal data outside India).

**Correction to the dates.** The clock runs from the **date of publication of the Gazette (14 Nov 2025)**, not from the 13 Nov 2025 notification date, so the milestones are **14 Nov 2026** (consent managers) and **14 May 2027** (substantive obligations) — not 13 Nov 2026 / 13 May 2027 as previously stated here. Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas states this explicitly: "The substantive provisions of the DPDP Act and DPDP Rules come into force in 18 months, i.e., 14 May 2027" (https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/). Some secondary commentary circulates 13 May 2027; it is off by one day and should not be used in client planning. Earlier caveat resolved: the rule numbers are no longer UNVERIFIED — the gazette PDF was successfully text-extracted (https://www.dpdpa.com/DPDP_Rules_2025_English_only.pdf, mirroring G.S.R. 846(E)), though meity.gov.in still returns HTTP 403 to automated fetching. Operationally: registration microsites, badge scans, photo/video capture and post-event analytics all become notice-and-consent surfaces against a hard **14 May 2027** date.

**EU / Germany — AI Act.** In force 1 Aug 2024; Article 5 prohibitions applied from 2 Feb 2025 (https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai). The commercially decisive nuance: Article 5(f) bans inferring emotions of natural persons **only** "in the areas of workplace and education institutions", with a medical/safety exception (https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/). An internal town hall or employee conference is a workplace — sentiment/emotion analytics on employee audiences in the EU is prohibited, while the same tool at a public consumer activation is not caught by 5(f) (though GDPR Art. 9 and 5(g) biometric-categorisation limits still bite). **This interpretation is now backed by the regulator, not just inference.** The Commission's own *Guidelines on prohibited artificial intelligence practices established by Regulation (EU) 2024/1689*, approved 4 Feb 2025 (https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-prohibited-artificial-intelligence-ai-practices-defined-ai-act), draw exactly this worker/customer line: the prohibition covers inferring *workers'* emotions but not *customers'* emotions, with the worked example that a voice-recognition system tracking customer emotions in a call centre falls outside scope while the same system pointed at the call-centre employees falls inside it. The guidelines also list prohibited modalities — inferring emotion from keystroke patterns, facial expressions, body posture or movement, webcams and voice — which is squarely the feature set event-engagement vendors market. Note the guidelines are formally non-binding, and fines for prohibited practices have been enforceable since 2 Aug 2025. Article 5(g) separately bans inferring race, political opinion, union membership, religion, sex life or sexual orientation from biometric data — which rules out most "audience demographic profiling from camera feeds" products. High-risk obligations were deferred by the Digital Omnibus political agreement of 7 May 2026 to 2 Dec 2027 (Annex III standalone, a 16-month deferral from 2 Aug 2026) and 2 Aug 2028 (Annex I embedded, a 12-month deferral from 2 Aug 2027), but this takes legal effect only on publication in the Official Journal, expected before 2 Aug 2026 (https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/). The 7 May 2026 agreement date and both deferred deadlines are corroborated by a second independent law-firm source (Covington, https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/eu-ai-act-update-timeline-relief-targeted-simplification-and-new-prohibitions/); still no Official Journal text, so the deferral remains **provisional pending formal adoption** — plan to the original dates for anything with a long build lead. Two points that matter for the hard rule above: (i) the Omnibus does **not** amend the existing Article 5 prohibitions, so 5(f) and 5(g) are unchanged and remain applicable from 2 Feb 2025 — the emotion-analytics ban is *not* deferred along with the high-risk regime, which is the trap a client will walk into; (ii) the Omnibus *adds* two new Article 5 prohibitions (non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM) from 2 Dec 2026, neither relevant to event delivery but both worth knowing if a client runs generative-AI content activations.

**China.** CAC's Provisions on Regulating and Promoting Cross-border Data Transfers (22 Mar 2024) created safe harbours most corporate events fall inside: non-sensitive personal data of fewer than 100,000 individuals per year, HR-necessary employee data, and data necessary to perform a contract with the individual (https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2024/china/what-you-need-to-know-about-chinas-new-data-export-rules, https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2024/3/china-relaxes-requirements-for-cross-border-data-transfers). Sensitive personal data of 10,000+ individuals per year crosses into assessment territory. **Important qualification the summary above understated:** sensitive personal data below that 10,000 threshold is *not* safe-harboured either — it requires standard contractual clauses plus a personal-information protection impact assessment filing. The full ladder per Greenberg Traurig is: non-sensitive PI under 100,000/yr exempt; 100,000 to under 1M/yr → SCCs + PIA filing; 1M+/yr → CAC security assessment; sensitive PI under 10,000/yr → SCCs + PIA filing; sensitive PI 10,000+/yr → CAC security assessment. For event delivery this bites earlier than the headline suggests, because dietary, medical, accessibility and biometric badge data are sensitive PI — a single 500-delegate conference collecting dietary requirements is already in SCC-plus-filing territory, not inside the safe harbour. (Official English rendering of the title is *Provisions on Promoting and Regulating the Cross-border Flow of Data*, effective immediately on issue, 22 Mar 2024.) On smart venues, the operative document I located is the sector standard 体育场馆智慧化评价规范 (Smart Stadium Evaluation Specification) under the General Administration of Sport, drafted with the National Sports Information Center and Huawei (https://std.samr.gov.cn/hb/search/stdHBDetailed?id=20BB2EE9B8DC9688E06397BE0A0AA025). No GB/T national standard for smart *exhibition* venues was found: **UNVERIFIED**.

**Japan — Society 5.0.** Introduced in the 5th Science and Technology Basic Plan (22 Jan 2016), defined by the Cabinet Office as "a human-centered society in which economic development and the resolution of social issues are compatible with each other through a highly integrated system of cyberspace and physical space", refined in the 6th Basic Plan (Mar 2021) around resilience, safety and diverse well-being (https://www8.cao.go.jp/cstp/english/society5_0/index.html). A procurement narrative rather than a rulebook — but Japanese corporate buyers respond to its vocabulary.

**UAE.** ePermits is the compliance chokepoint (§1); Expo City's Green Innovation District designation ties event bids to sustainability reporting (https://www.expocitydubai.com/en/).

## 5. Transferable to an India-based enterprise event integrator

1. **Zero-install attendee journeys.** WeChat's mini-program model works because identity, payment and messaging already exist in-channel. India's analogue is WhatsApp + UPI, not an app download; the WEF/Tencent Davos build is the proof of concept (https://www.tencent.com/en-us/articles/2202375.html).
2. **Organiser-owned matchmaking as a product line.** HKTDC monetises Click2Match/Scan2Match as part of the fair, not as a vendor add-on. An integrator owning the matchmaking layer across a client's portfolio captures the data asset.
3. **Pre-emptive GDPR-style data posture.** Converve and vivenu sell on data ownership and DPAs. With DPDP substantive obligations landing 14 May 2027 (corrected from 13 May — see §4), consent-manager-ready registration built now is a two-year head start.
4. **Passive WiFi occupancy estimation over app-based tracking** (DOI 10.1109/secon68281.2026.11578441) — hall-level footfall without instrumenting attendees, avoiding DPDP consent burden.
5. **RFID zone analytics with an explicit evacuation use case.** Gazis & Katsiri (DOI 10.3390/s24113643) pair footfall analytics with a safety justification — the strongest available consent and internal-approval argument.
6. **Edge-first video processing** (DOI 10.3390/jsan15010015). For Indian venues with unreliable uplinks this is operationally necessary, and it shrinks the data-transfer surface.
7. **Hard rule: no emotion analytics on employee audiences.** EU Art. 5(f) prohibits it outright; applying the same rule globally removes a class of client legal escalation (see DOI 10.1007/s43681-023-00307-3).
8. **A separate Japanese-language registration and comms layer**, not localisation of a global tool — that is how the market buys (https://eventhub.jp/blog/event-platform-comparison/).
9. **Digital permitting discipline.** Dubai's mandatory ePermit-before-E-Code sequencing is a template for putting permit dependency on the critical path instead of a parallel workstream.
10. **Venue digital twins for pre-event planning, not spectator experience** — the Paris 2024-anchored literature (DOI 10.3389/fspor.2023.1289140) evidences planning value; spectator-facing twin claims are not evidenced.
11. **Platform business-model thinking for recurring client events** (DOI 10.1016/j.jhtm.2026.101470): year-round engagement and monetisation designed in, not a microsite that dies at load-out.
12. **Design hybrid formats for psychological safety, and disclose the sensing.** Constantinides & Quercia (arXiv:2205.04977) show engagement measurement sliding into surveillance; disclosure is both ethics and client trust.
